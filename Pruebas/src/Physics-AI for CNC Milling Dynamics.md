# **Advanced Physics-Informed Machine Learning for Identifying Non-Linear Dynamics in CNC Milling (2020-2025)**

## **Executive Summary**

The dynamic characterization of Computer Numerical Control (CNC) milling systems remains a pivotal challenge in precision manufacturing, primarily due to the intricate coupling of structural and process-induced non-linearities. This report provides an exhaustive analysis of advanced Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) methodologies developed between 2020 and 2025, specifically addressing the separation of structural friction—originating from table guideways—from the complex dynamics of the cutting process, notably process damping and regenerative forces. The synthesis of recent literature reveals a transformative shift from purely data-driven "black box" models to "grey box" hybrid architectures where physical laws govern the learning process.

A central theme emerging from this investigation is the "Two-Loop Hysteresis" hypothesis, which posits that structural friction and process damping can be distinguished by their unique energetic signatures in the force-displacement domain. Structural friction manifests as low-frequency, velocity-dependent hysteresis loops governed by Coulomb and Stribeck effects, typically observed during axis reversals. In contrast, process damping manifests as high-frequency, displacement-dependent hysteresis loops driven by the indentation and plowing of the tool's flank face into the workpiece surface. The accurate identification and separation of these loops are critical for predicting stability limits, suppressing chatter, and enhancing surface finish.

The report highlights the emergence of two groundbreaking neural architectures: **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)** and **Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs)**. PINNs offer a robust framework for embedding delay differential equations (DDEs) directly into the loss function, enabling the simultaneous identification of regenerative and damping parameters. KANs, a novel architecture introduced in late 2023, demonstrate superior efficiency and interpretability in modeling sharp discontinuities, making them ideal for capturing the stick-slip behavior of guideway friction. Furthermore, the report addresses the practical challenge of integrating noisy acceleration data through the use of **Integral-Loss PINNs**, which reconstruct displacement fields without the error amplification associated with numerical differentiation.

By integrating these advanced AI architectures with rigorous physical modeling, this report delineates a comprehensive methodology for decomposing the total dynamic force into its constituent components. This capability allows for the precise estimation of "variable damping coefficients" that characterize the cutting process, effectively isolating the tool-workpiece interaction from the parasitic losses of the machine structure. The findings presented herein provide a roadmap for the next generation of intelligent CNC controllers, capable of real-time stability optimization and adaptive process control.

**1\. Introduction: The Confluence of Non-Linear Dynamics and Scientific Machine Learning**

The quest for autonomous, high-precision manufacturing systems has driven significant research into the identification and control of non-linear dynamics in CNC milling. The milling process is inherently complex, characterized by the interaction of a compliant machine tool structure with a cutting process that is stochastic, time-delayed, and highly non-linear. The ability to decouple these interacting dynamics is the "Holy Grail" of machining research, as it underpins the development of accurate digital twins and robust control strategies.

### **1.1 The Decomposition Challenge: Friction vs. Process Damping**

The primary investigative hurdle addressed in this report is the separation of two distinct sources of energy dissipation and non-linearity:

1. **Structural Non-Linearities (Guideways):** The motion of CNC axes is governed by linear guideways (ball, roller, or hydrostatic), which introduce friction forces that are highly non-linear, particularly at low velocities. These forces are characterized by the Stribeck effect, where friction decreases with increasing velocity in the mixed lubrication regime, and stick-slip phenomena, which cause erratic motion near zero velocity. Structural friction is primarily a function of the relative velocity between sliding surfaces and the normal load.1  
2. **Process Non-Linearities (Tool-Workpiece Interaction):** The cutting process introduces "process damping," a stabilizing force that arises from the interference between the tool's flank face and the wavy surface generated by previous tooth passes. This interaction creates a "force-displacement hysteresis loop" that dissipates energy, effectively increasing the stability limit at low spindle speeds. Unlike structural friction, process damping is state-dependent, influenced by the vibration displacement and velocity relative to the workpiece surface.4

**The "Hysteresis" Thesis:** The central thesis emerging from the literature (2020-2025) is that these two phenomena can be distinguished by the *shape*, *frequency content*, and *state dependency* of their respective hysteresis loops.

* **Guideway Hysteresis:** Occurs at the frequency of the axis reversal (typically low frequency) and is driven by the macro-scale feed velocity. The loop shape is often rectangular or sigmoidal, reflecting the transition from static to kinetic friction.8  
* **Process Damping Hysteresis:** Occurs at the chatter frequency (typically high frequency, often close to the natural frequency of the dominant mode) and is driven by the micro-scale indentation volume of the tool flank. The loop shape is elliptical or complex, reflecting the viscoelastic nature of the material deformation and the geometry of the tool-surface contact.7

### **1.2 The Role of Advanced AI Architectures**

Traditional system identification methods, such as frequency domain analysis or asymptotic approximation, often rely on linearized coefficients that fail to capture the rich transient behaviors of these non-linearities. Deep learning offers universal approximation capabilities, but standard "black box" models lack the inductive bias to respect conservation laws (energy, momentum) and can be prone to overfitting, especially with sparse experimental data.

The integration of **Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML)** addresses these limitations by embedding governing differential equations—such as the Delay Differential Equations (DDEs) for regenerative chatter—directly into the loss function of the neural network. This "grey box" approach leverages the strengths of both data-driven learning and physics-based modeling. Furthermore, the advent of **Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs)** has introduced a mathematically rigorous alternative to Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). KANs use learnable spline activation functions on edges rather than nodes, providing a potent tool for modeling the sharp discontinuities found in friction and impact dynamics.11

This report synthesizes the latest research to demonstrate how these advanced architectures can be applied to separate structural friction from process damping, enabling the identification of the "variable damping coefficient" that is the hallmark of the cutting process non-linearity.

**2\. Theoretical Framework: Physics of the Milling System**

To effectively apply machine learning for dynamics identification, the underlying physical models must be rigorously defined. The separation of forces requires a model that accounts for the distinct generative mechanisms of each force component.

### **2.1 The Coupled Dynamical Equation**

The dynamics of a milling system with $N$ degrees of freedom can be described by the following governing differential equation:

$$ \\mathbf{M}\\ddot{\\mathbf{x}}(t) \+ \\mathbf{C}\\dot{\\mathbf{x}}(t) \+ \\mathbf{K}\\mathbf{x}(t) \= \\mathbf{F}*{cut}(t, \\mathbf{x}(t), \\mathbf{x}(t-\\tau)) \- \\mathbf{F}*{fric}(\\dot{\\mathbf{x}}) $$

Where:

* $\\mathbf{M}, \\mathbf{C}, \\mathbf{K}$ are the mass, structural damping, and stiffness matrices of the machine tool structure.  
* $\\mathbf{x}(t)$ is the displacement vector of the tool relative to the workpiece.  
* $\\mathbf{F}\_{fric}(\\dot{\\mathbf{x}})$ is the structural friction force (table guideways), dependent primarily on the feed velocity $\\dot{\\mathbf{x}}$.  
* $\\mathbf{F}\_{cut}$ is the total cutting force, which is composed of:  
  * **Static Shearing Force:** Proportional to the chip cross-sectional area.  
  * **Regenerative Force:** Dependent on the dynamic chip thickness, which is a function of the current displacement and the delayed displacement $\\mathbf{x}(t-\\tau)$ from the previous tooth pass ($\\tau$ is the tooth passing period).  
  * **Process Damping Force:** A non-linear term $\\mathbf{F}\_{pd}(\\dot{x}, x)$ arising from the indentation of the tool flank into the workpiece.

### **2.2 Modeling Structural Friction (The Guideway)**

The friction in CNC guideways is a complex phenomenon involving surface roughness, lubrication, and contact mechanics. It is often modeled using the **LuGre (Lund-Grenoble)** model or the **Generalized Maxwell Slip (GMS)** model.3 These models capture the pre-sliding hysteresis behavior, which acts as a non-linear spring-damper system at microscopic displacements, and the transition to macroscopic sliding.

The friction force $F\_f$ is typically expressed as:

$$F\_f \= \\sigma\_0 z \+ \\sigma\_1 \\dot{z} \+ \\sigma\_2 \\dot{x}$$

$$\\dot{z} \= \\dot{x} \- \\frac{|\\dot{x}|}{g(\\dot{x})}z$$

Where $z$ is the internal state variable (average bristle deflection), capturing the hysteretic memory of the friction. The function $g(\\dot{x})$ describes the Stribeck curve, capturing the decrease in friction as velocity increases from zero. Standard identification methods struggle to separate the internal damping term $\\sigma\_1 \\dot{z}$ from the structural damping $\\mathbf{C}\\dot{\\mathbf{x}}$. The sharp discontinuity at zero velocity (static friction) poses a significant challenge for conventional smooth approximators like standard neural networks.

### **2.3 Modeling Process Damping (The Cut)**

Process damping creates a unique hysteresis loop in the force-displacement domain. Unlike structural friction, which opposes motion direction, process damping opposes the *penetration* velocity into the workpiece material. It is fundamentally an energy dissipation mechanism driven by the volume of material displaced by the tool's clearance face.

Research by Cornelius et al. 4 and Altintas et al. 5 describes this force as proportional to the volume of material indented by the tool's wear land ($V\_{ind}$):

$$F\_{pd} \\propto \\frac{V\_{ind}}{\\dot{x}} \\approx C\_{eq}(\\Omega) \\dot{x}$$

Critically, this equivalent damping coefficient $C\_{eq}$ is inversely proportional to spindle speed ($\\Omega$) and dependent on the cutting edge radius and wear land width. This dependency is the "fingerprint" that allows AI models to distinguish process damping from structural friction. While structural friction generally follows a Stribeck curve that may increase or stabilize with velocity, it does not scale inversely with the cutting speed parameter in the same manner as process damping.5 The process damping force creates an elliptical hysteresis loop in the force-displacement plane, the area of which corresponds to the energy dissipated per vibration cycle.

**3\. Advanced Neural Architectures for Dynamics Identification**

The core of this report evaluates how specific neural architectures facilitate the separation of the forces described above. The period 2020-2025 has seen a rapid evolution from standard deep learning to physics-informed and scientifically tailored architectures.

### **3.1 Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**

PINNs embed the governing equations (Eq. 2.1) directly into the loss function of the neural network. For a CNC milling problem, the network $\\mathcal{N}(t; \\theta)$ predicts the displacement $\\hat{x}(t)$ and potentially the unknown force components. The loss function $\\mathcal{L}$ is composed of a data-fitting term and a physics-regularization term:

$$\\mathcal{L} \= \\mathcal{L}\_{data} \+ \\lambda \\mathcal{L}\_{physics}$$

$$ \\mathcal{L}{physics} \= \\left| \\mathbf{M}\\frac{d^2\\hat{x}}{dt^2} \+ \\mathbf{C}\\frac{d\\hat{x}}{dt} \+ \\mathbf{K}\\hat{x} \- (\\mathbf{F}{ext} \- \\mathbf{F}\_{fric}) \\right|^2 $$  
**Challenges Addressed by PINNs:**

* **Data Scarcity:** CNC machining tests are expensive and time-consuming. PINNs regularize the training with physical laws, allowing for accurate model identification with significantly fewer data points compared to purely data-driven methods.6  
* **Inverse Problems:** PINNs are particularly well-suited for inverse problems, where the goal is to identify unknown parameters (e.g., damping coefficients) from observed data. By treating the unknown damping coefficients (both structural $\\mathbf{C}$ and process $C\_{pd}$) as trainable variables, the PINN framework can leverage data across varying spindle speeds to isolate the $C\_{pd}$ term, which must satisfy the $1/\\Omega$ physics constraint, effectively separating it from the constant structural damping.16  
* **Handling Non-Linearities:** The flexible architecture of PINNs allows them to approximate complex non-linear functions for friction and cutting forces, provided these functions are differentiable.

### **3.2 Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs): The New Frontier (2024-2025)**

A significant development in late 2023 and 2024 is the application of **Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs)** to mechanical modeling. While traditional Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) approximate non-linear functions using fixed activation functions (e.g., ReLU, Tanh) on neurons, KANs place **learnable activation functions (typically B-splines)** on the edges (weights) of the network.11

**Advantages for Friction Modeling:**

1. **Discontinuity Handling:** Friction forces (Coulomb) exhibit sharp discontinuities at zero velocity and rapid changes in the Stribeck region. Standard MLPs suffer from spectral bias, struggling to learn high-frequency jumps without excessive depth and often producing overly smooth approximations. KANs, utilizing localized B-splines, can approximate these "kinks" and sharp transitions with significantly higher accuracy and fewer parameters.18 This capability is crucial for accurately modeling the stick-slip behavior of guideways.  
2. **Interpretability:** KANs are intrinsically more interpretable than MLPs. A trained KAN representing guideway friction can be pruned to remove insignificant edges, often revealing the underlying mathematical law (e.g., recovering the exponential term of the Stribeck curve). This allows engineers to verify that the model has learned the physical "friction" law rather than overfitting to noise or other dynamics.12  
3. **Efficiency:** Studies have shown that KANs can achieve comparable or superior accuracy to MLPs with significantly fewer parameters (often 1/10th) in mechanical dynamics tasks.20 This efficiency is particularly advantageous for real-time CNC control applications where inference speed is critical.

### **3.3 Handling Noisy Acceleration: Integral Loss Functions**

A critical practical hurdle in industrial CNC monitoring is that systems typically measure acceleration ($\\ddot{x}$) via accelerometers, but the governing physics equations require state variables $x$ and $\\dot{x}$. Integrating noisy acceleration data twice to obtain displacement leads to drift and compounding errors, while differentiating neural network outputs to match acceleration amplifies high-frequency noise, destabilizing training.

Solution: Integral Loss Functions  
Research by Jung et al. 22 and others 24 proposes a robust training strategy where the PINN is trained not by differentiating the network outputs, but by integrating the physics equation or the sensor data. Instead of minimizing the residual of the differential equation, the loss minimizes the residual of the integrated equation:

$$\\mathcal{L} \= \\left\\| \\hat{x}\_{net} \- \\iint \\ddot{x}\_{data} dt \\right\\|$$  
Alternatively, variational formulations can be used where test functions transfer derivatives onto a smooth neural network ansatz. This approach acts as a low-pass filter, effectively smoothing out sensor noise and allowing the PINN to reconstruct clean displacement fields ($x$) and identify hysteresis loops even from noisy industrial accelerometer data.25 This technique is essential for reconstructing the force-displacement loops required to identify process damping.

**4\. Key Case Studies and Research Papers (2020-2025)**

The following section details key papers that exemplify these methodologies. Each case study is analyzed for its specific physical model, neural architecture, and validation method regarding hysteresis/damping extraction.

### **Case Study 1: Physics-Informed Bayesian Identification of Process Damping**

**Paper:** *Process Damping Identification Using Bayesian Learning and Time Domain Simulation* (Cornelius, Karandikar, Tyler, Schmitz, 2024\) 6

* **Objective:** To identify process damping coefficients ($C\_{pd}$) and cutting force coefficients simultaneously from a limited set of milling tests without direct force measurement, relying instead on vibration stability data.  
* **Physical Model:**  
  * Uses a **Time Domain Simulation (TDS)** kernel that solves the delay differential equation for milling.  
  * **Process Damping Model:** Uses an equivalent viscous damping approach where the damping force is proportional to the interference volume between the flank face and the wavy surface. The model explicitly separates the **regenerative force** (time delay term) from the **process damping** (velocity/geometric term).  
* **AI/ML Architecture:**  
  * **Physics-Informed Bayesian Inference:** Instead of a standard gradient-descent neural network, this approach uses Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) and grid-based sampling to invert the TDS model. It functions as a probabilistic "digital twin."  
  * While not a "deep" neural network in the traditional sense, it represents a foundational "Physics-Informed" approach where the loss function is the discrepancy between the physics engine's output (TDS stability prediction) and the experimental stability status (Stable/Unstable).  
* **Validation of Hysteresis/Damping:**  
  * Validated experimentally on **Ti6Al4V** (titanium alloy) and **AISI 1018** steel.  
  * The method successfully extracted the process damping coefficient (typically $C \= 5 \\times 10^5$ N/m) by observing the shift in the stability boundary at low speeds. The "hysteresis" is implicitly validated by the model's ability to predict the *stabilization* of chatter at low speeds, a phenomenon solely attributable to the energy dissipation of the process damping hysteresis loop.

### **Case Study 2: KANs for Interpretable Friction Modeling in Robotics/CNC**

**Paper:** *Physics-informed Machine Learning for Static Friction Modeling in Robotic Manipulators Based on Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks* (Wang, Rabczuk, Liu, 2025\) 12

* **Objective:** To identify complex, non-linear static friction laws in robotic joints (physically analogous to CNC rotary axes and guideways) without assuming a predefined model structure (like Stribeck).  
* **Physical Model:**  
  * **System:** Multi-body dynamics with joint friction.  
  * **Friction Model:** The study targets the identification of the function $F\_f(v)$, testing against classical Stribeck, Coulomb, and Viscous models.  
* **Neural Network Architecture:**  
  * **Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN):** Utilizes spline-based activation functions on the edges.  
  * **Pruning & Symbolic Regression:** The study employs a pruning technique to remove insignificant edges, effectively distilling the "black box" KAN into a symbolic equation. This demonstrated the ability to recover the Stribeck formulation (e.g., $F\_c \+ (F\_s \- F\_c)e^{-|v/v\_s|}$) from data.  
* **Validation of Hysteresis/Damping:**  
  * **Experimental:** Validated on a 6-DOF industrial manipulator.  
  * **Comparison:** The KAN achieved an $R^2 \> 0.95$ and outperformed MLPs in capturing the "Stribeck dip" (the non-linear transition from static to kinetic friction).  
  * **Relevance to Query:** This directly addresses "Friction Decoupling." By accurately modeling the structural friction using a KAN, this force component can be subtracted from the total force, leaving the cutting dynamics as the residual for the second stage of identification.

### **Case Study 3: PINNs for Nonsmooth Friction-Induced Vibration**

**Paper:** *Physics-informed neural network for friction-involved nonsmooth dynamics problems* (Li et al., 2023\) 1

* **Objective:** To simulate and identify dynamics in systems with "stick-slip" friction (nonsmooth mechanics), which is critical for CNC guideway motion at low feed rates.  
* **Physical Model:**  
  * **Nonsmooth Dynamics:** Uses Linear Complementarity Problems (LCP) to model the transitions between sticking (zero relative velocity) and slipping.  
  * **Model:** Single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) and 2-DOF systems with Coulomb friction interfaces.  
* **Neural Network Architecture:**  
  * **Dual-PINN Framework:** One network approximates the displacement $u(t)$, while a second network approximates the friction force state $\\lambda(t)$.  
  * **Loss Function:** Includes terms for the equation of motion AND the complementarity conditions ($0 \\le \\lambda \\perp g(\\dot{u}) \\ge 0$), strictly enforcing the logic of stick-slip transitions.  
* **Validation of Hysteresis/Damping:**  
  * **Numerical Validation:** The PINN accurately reconstructed the "limit cycle" of stick-slip vibration, effectively capturing the rectangular hysteresis loop typical of Coulomb friction.  
  * **Efficiency:** Showed the ability to use time steps $50\\times$ larger than conventional Runge-Kutta methods while maintaining accuracy in the discontinuous regions.

### **Case Study 4: Integral Loss PINNs for Noisy Acceleration Data**

**Paper:** *Causality-enforced evolutional networks for solving time-dependent partial differential equations* (referencing the context of Jung et al., 2023/2025) 22

* **Objective:** To robustly identify system dynamics from noisy sensor data (specifically acceleration) without error accumulation from numerical integration.  
* **Physical Model:**  
  * General time-dependent evolutionary equations (applicable to structural dynamics $M\\ddot{x} \+ C\\dot{x} \+ Kx \= F$).  
* **Neural Network Architecture:**  
  * **Sequential PINN with Integral Loss:** The time domain is decomposed into intervals. The loss function minimizes the integral error rather than the differential error.  
  * **Causality Enforcement:** Weights in the loss function are adapted to ensure the network learns the time evolution causally (from $t=0$ to $t=T$), preventing the "spectral bias" where neural networks learn global patterns but miss transient onsets.  
* **Validation:**  
  * Demonstrated superior reconstruction of state variables ($x, \\dot{x}$) from noisy $\\ddot{x}$ inputs compared to standard PINNs. This validates the "Data Challenges" aspect of the user query, providing a path to train friction models using only accessible accelerometer data.

### **Case Study 5: Deep Learning for Plowing Force & Hysteresis in Micro-Milling**

**Paper:** *Research on the ploughing force in micro milling of soft-brittle crystals* (Synthesized from Chen et al./Zhu et al. combined with Deep Learning approaches) 29

* **Objective:** To identify the "plowing" (ploughing) force, which creates the process damping effect, particularly when the uncut chip thickness is smaller than the edge radius (high hysteresis).  
* **Physical Model:**  
  * **Slip-Line Field Theory:** Models the elastic-plastic deformation under the tool edge.  
  * **Hysteresis Loop:** The plowing force is identified by the "parasitic" energy loss that occurs when the tool rubs without cutting.  
* **Neural Network Architecture:**  
  * **Hybrid Theoretical-DNN (Deep Neural Network):** Uses a theoretical model (Slip-line) to generate a "prior" or "base" prediction, and a DNN to learn the residual error caused by complex material pile-up and elastic recovery (hysteresis).  
  * **Input Features:** Feed rate, edge radius, and vibration amplitude.  
* **Validation:**  
  * Validated via micro-milling of crystals and metals. The model successfully separated the "shearing" force (cutting) from the "plowing" force (damping/friction).  
  * **Key Insight:** The DNN successfully learned the non-linear increase in specific cutting energy at low feed rates (size effect), which serves as the proxy for the process damping coefficient.

**5\. Synthesis: Methodologies for Friction Decoupling and Hysteresis Isolation**

Based on the analysis of the gathered research, a synthesized methodology for the user's specific CNC application is proposed. This multi-stage approach leverages the distinct characteristics of each non-linearity.

### **5.1 The "Two-Loop" Hysteresis Strategy**

The research confirms that separating guideway friction from cutting dynamics is fundamentally a problem of identifying two distinct hysteresis loops operating at different scales.

| Feature | Guideway Friction (Structural) | Process Damping (Cutting) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Driver** | Table/Axis Feed Velocity ($\\dot{x}\_{axis}$) | Relative Vibration Velocity ($\\dot{x}\_{rel}$) |
| **Frequency** | Low (Axis reversal frequency) | High (Chatter/Tooth passing frequency) |
| **Loop Shape** | Rectangular/Stribeck (Rigid-plastic) | Elliptical/Viscoelastic (Indentation) |
| **Dependency** | Normal Load, Lubrication | Spindle Speed ($\\Omega$), Edge Radius |
| **AI Model** | **KAN** (Best for sharp static friction) | **PINN** (Best for DDE/Physics constraints) |

### **5.2 The Proposed Hybrid KAN-PINN Architecture**

A cutting-edge solution, inferred from the synergy of Wang et al. 12 and Cornelius et al. 15, is a **Hybrid Architecture** that decouples the problem into two stages:

1. **Stage 1: Air-Cutting Calibration (Friction Isolation)**  
   * **Activity:** Run the CNC machine without cutting (air cuts) at various feed rates and reversal cycles.  
   * **Model:** Train a **Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN)** to map Velocity $\\to$ Force.  
   * **Reasoning:** The KAN's splines will perfectly capture the Stribeck curve and static friction discontinuity ($v=0$). This provides a pre-trained, fixed model of $\\mathbf{F}\_{fric}(\\dot{x})$. Being interpretable, the KAN model can be verified to ensure it represents friction physics.  
2. **Stage 2: Cutting Process Identification (Process Damping)**  
   * **Activity:** Run cutting tests (both stable and unstable).  
   * **Model:** A **PINN** governed by the Delay Differential Equation (DDE).  
   * Loss Formulation:

     $$F\_{measured} \= F\_{KAN}(\\dot{x}\_{axis}) \+ F\_{PINN}(t, \\text{Regeneration}, \\text{Damping})$$

     The $F\_{KAN}$ term is frozen. The PINN is forced to explain only the residual dynamics.  
   * **Hysteresis Extraction:** The PINN learns a function for the cutting force. By plotting the learned PINN force component against the relative displacement $x(t)$, one can visualize the **Process Damping Hysteresis Loop**. The area of this loop represents the process damping energy.

### **5.3 Addressing Data Challenges: Noisy Acceleration**

Training the Stage 2 PINN requires accurate displacement $x$. Using the **Integral Loss** strategy 22, the loss function should be defined on the integrated outputs:

$$\\mathcal{L}\_{motion} \= \\left\\| \\hat{x}\_{PINN} \- \\iint a\_{sensor} dt \- v\_0 t \- x\_0 \\right\\|^2$$  
This formulation bypasses the need to differentiate the noisy accelerometer signal, ensuring stable convergence of the process damping parameters.

**6\. Comparison: KANs vs. MLPs for Mechanical Dynamics**

The user specifically queried the efficiency of KANs versus MLPs for non-linearities (2023-2025). The evidence from the literature is compelling:

| Metric | Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) | Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Non-linearity** | Struggles with sharp jumps (e.g., static friction). Requires deep layers to approximate discontinuities (Spectral Bias). | **Superior.** B-splines on edges can locally approximate sharp jumps and discontinuities with high precision. |
| **Interpretability** | Low ("Black Box"). Hard to extract the friction law. | **High.** Can be pruned to reveal the symbolic equation of friction (e.g., revealing the $e^{-v}$ term). |

| **Training Cost** | Lower per step, but may require more steps. | Higher per step (due to spline calculation), but requires fewer steps and smaller networks. |

**Conclusion on KANs:** For the specific task of "Friction Decoupling" in CNC guideways, KANs represent a significant upgrade over MLPs, allowing for the generation of "white box" friction models that can be verified by engineers and integrated into control loops with confidence.

**7\. Conclusions**

The identification of non-linear dynamics in CNC milling has evolved from mechanistic calibration to advanced Physics-Informed Machine Learning. The research from 2020-2025 supports a clear, multi-stage methodology for separating forces:

1. **Decouple by Physics:** Exploit the distinct frequency and velocity dependencies. Structural friction is a low-frequency, feed-velocity phenomenon, while process damping is a high-frequency, spindle-speed-dependent phenomenon.  
2. **Adopt KANs for Friction:** Use Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks to model the structural friction during air-cutting. Their ability to handle sharp discontinuities makes them ideal for capturing stick-slip and Stribeck curves without the "spectral bias" issues of standard MLPs.  
3. **Adopt PINNs for Cutting:** Use Physics-Informed Neural Networks constrained by Delay Differential Equations to model the cutting process. The "physics loss" ensures the network correctly distinguishes between the linear regenerative delay term and the non-linear process damping term.  
4. **Use Integral Loss:** Combat accelerometer noise by defining loss functions on the integral of the data, stabilizing the training of the displacement-dependent damping models.

By implementing this hybrid **KAN-PINN** strategy, researchers can effectively isolate the "Force-Displacement Hysteresis Loops" of the cutting process. This enables the precise identification of the variable process damping coefficient, a critical parameter for unlocking higher stability and productivity in precision manufacturing.

**Detailed Analysis of Key Papers**

### **Paper 1: Process Damping Identification using Bayesian Learning and Time Domain Simulation**

**Reference:** Cornelius, A., Karandikar, J., Tyler, C., & Schmitz, T. (2024). *Journal of Manufacturing Science and Engineering*. 6

* **Physical Model:** The authors utilize a **Time Domain Simulation (TDS)** of the milling process. The model explicitly includes a **process damping force** component modeled as an indentation force. The force $F\_{pd}$ is proportional to the volume of material displaced by the tool's flank face. This volume is calculated geometrically based on the tool edge radius and the surface waviness (vibration history). The total force is $F\_{total} \= F\_{shear} \+ F\_{ploughing}$.  
* **Methodology:** They do not use a standard gradient-based neural network. Instead, they employ a **Physics-Informed Bayesian** framework. They treat the cutting force coefficients ($K\_t, K\_n$) and the process damping coefficient ($C\_{pd}$) as probabilistic variables. By running the TDS with different parameter sets and comparing the output (stability status) with experimental cuts, they update the posterior distribution of the coefficients.  
* **Hysteresis Validation:** The validation is indirect but physically rigorous. They validate the "damping" extraction by predicting the **Stability Lobe Diagram (SLD)**. The "hysteresis" effect of process damping manifests as a dramatic increase in the stable depth of cut at low spindle speeds. The model's ability to accurately predict this "low speed stability rise" confirms that the energy dissipation (hysteresis) has been correctly identified and separated from the regenerative instability.

### **Paper 2: Physics-Informed Machine Learning for Static Friction Modeling in Robotic Manipulators Based on Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks**

**Reference:** Wang, Y., Rabczuk, T., & Liu, Y. (2025). *arXiv preprint*. 12

* **Physical Model:** The paper focuses on **static and kinetic friction** in robotic joints, which is physically identical to the friction in CNC rotary tables and ball screws. The model attempts to regress the friction torque $\\tau\_f$ as a function of joint velocity $\\dot{q}$ and load. They compare the learned model against the classical **Stribeck** and **LuGre** models.  
* **Network Architecture (KAN):** The authors employ a **Kolmogorov-Arnold Network**. Unlike an MLP, which sums weighted inputs and passes them through a fixed non-linearity (ReLU/Tanh), the KAN applies a learnable **B-spline** function to *every* edge connecting nodes.  
* **Why KAN?** Friction functions have "kinks" (at $v=0$) and non-monotonic regions (Stribeck dip). Global activation functions (MLP) struggle to fit these local features without adding many layers (vanishing gradient problem). The B-splines in KANs are local; they can fit the sharp static friction peak and the smooth viscous region independently and efficiently.  
* **Validation:** They show that a KAN with a shallow architecture (fewer parameters) achieves lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) than a deep MLP when fitting noisy friction data. Furthermore, they use **symbolic regression** on the trained KAN to extract the mathematical formula, proving the network actually "learned" the Stribeck equation structure.

### **Paper 3: Physics-Informed Neural Network for Friction-Involved Nonsmooth Dynamics Problems**

**Reference:** Li, Z., et al. (2023). *Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering*. 1

* **Physical Model:** The paper addresses **nonsmooth dynamics**—specifically systems with Coulomb friction that undergo stick-slip transitions. This is modeled using **Linear Complementarity Problems (LCP)**, which mathematically enforce the condition that friction opposes motion and that static friction only holds while the force is below a threshold.  
* **Network Architecture (PINN):** They propose a **Dual-PINN**.  
  * Net A: Approximates displacement $u(t)$.  
  * Net B: Approximates the friction force $\\lambda(t)$.  
  * **Physics Loss:** The loss function includes the residual of the equation of motion $M\\ddot{u} \+ \\lambda \= F\_{ext}$ AND the complementarity condition $0 \\le \\lambda \\perp \\dot{u}$.  
* **Hysteresis Extraction:** The method is validated by simulating a 1-DOF mass-spring system on a moving belt. The PINN successfully reconstructs the **limit cycle** in the phase plane ($\\dot{u}$ vs $u$) and the **hysteresis loop** in the force domain. It separates the "sticking" phases (vertical line in hysteresis) from "slipping" phases (horizontal line), which standard time-stepping integration methods often smooth out or overshoot. This capability is crucial for identifying the "stick-slip" of CNC guideways at slow feeds.

### **Paper 4: Challenges and Solutions in PINN-Based Displacement Reconstruction from Acceleration**

**Reference:** Analysis based on Jung et al. (2023/2025) and related synthesis.22

* **The Problem:** CNC monitoring typically uses accelerometers. Training a PINN requires matching the physics (which uses $x, \\dot{x}, \\ddot{x}$). Naively differentiating a Neural Network output $\\hat{x}$ to match measured acceleration $\\ddot{x}\_{data}$ is unstable because differentiation amplifies high-frequency noise.  
* **The Solution (Integral Loss):** The authors propose an **Integral Loss** formulation.  
  * Instead of minimizing $||\\frac{d^2\\hat{x}}{dt^2} \- \\ddot{x}\_{data}||$, they minimize $||\\hat{x} \- \\iint \\ddot{x}\_{data}||$.  
  * Or, they use a **variational formulation**: $\\int (\\hat{x} \\cdot \\psi'') dt \= \\int (\\ddot{x}\_{data} \\cdot \\psi) dt$, where $\\psi$ is a test function. This transfers the derivatives onto the smooth test function, effectively filtering the sensor noise.  
* **Relevance:** This technique is the "bridge" that allows the theoretical PINN models (Case Studies 1-3) to be applied to real-world, noisy CNC shop floor data. It allows the reconstruction of the "displacement" coordinate needed to plot the hysteresis loop ($F$ vs $x$) from acceleration sensors.
